01 · The life behind the name

Behind the Scars — Before the Heart Wakes

Not one wound. Not one label. Still here.

I kept seeing people treated like they were nothing.

People without a home being picked on. People struggling with addiction being laughed at. LGBTQ people getting torn apart for existing. Mental illness turned into a joke. Everybody pointing a finger while somebody else stood there taking the damage.

And I had plenty to say about it.

If I’d been there, I would’ve said something. I would’ve stood up for them. I’d find time to help at a shelter. Make a stand. Do something that mattered.

I kept saying it.

I got angry at the news, the schools, the government. Why the hell was this happening? Why was it allowed? People were supposed to be protected. Every one of them.

But I wasn’t doing shit about it.

That was hard to admit, because I really did care. I cared enough to get angry. Enough to hurt over it. But stepping forward meant giving people a reason to look at me.

My faults. My past. Everything they could pick apart.

There’s a lot in my life somebody could judge. There are also choices I have to own. What happened to me doesn’t erase my responsibility for what I’ve done. Telling my story means being honest about that, too.

I was afraid of being ridiculed. Afraid of starting fights. Afraid that trying to help somebody else would give the world another chance to tear me down.

So I sat there, caring, hurting, and complaining.

Eventually, I had to face how little that was changing.

My first idea was a website where people could write how they felt. Looking back, I thought it was dumb. I spent a year struggling to build apps and figure out social media, failing at things I wanted so badly to get right.

Then I finished Axiomara. And I came up with GhostHeart.

A way to hide and still tell my story.

GhostHeart carries the bravery, the strength, the courage I’m still trying to find in myself. He gives me a way to step forward while part of me still wants to stay hidden.

I’m the man behind him. The one who cares deeply and is still scared.

That’s where the awakening began: admitting that I could spend my life being angry about people’s pain without ever doing anything useful with my own.

Maybe my story could help somebody. Even with the ugly parts. Even with the mistakes I have to own. Even if the world judged me for telling it.

I don’t know how many people will read these words. I don’t know if I’ll ever stop being afraid of what comes next.

But if I help one person, then I did something.

I finally did more than sit there and wish somebody would.

02 · The relationship behind the work

The Woman Behind the Scenes.

Her love helped shape the man he is today. Meet GhostHeart’s Rebel.

Read The Woman Behind the Scenes

I was confused about love.

Some people held hands and made it look easy. Others said they screamed because they cared. Some of what people called love left bruises. Some love left.

I didn’t understand how all of that could live under the same word.

But I knew what I wanted.

I wanted my grandparents’ love.

Married within six months. Kids. A house. Fifty-four years together. From where I stood, they always worked as a team. I didn’t see yelling or fighting. I saw two people who made loving each other look like the easiest thing in the world.

They were perfect to me.

I wanted that. I just didn’t understand that I could spend my life looking for their love and miss the love standing right in front of me.

Because I’m not my grandpa. And my life didn’t look like his.

I knew my momma loved me. I knew I could give somebody my whole heart. But giving everything I had didn’t mean I knew how to love well. I was still learning how to be the man I wanted to be. How to be a good husband. How to recognize love when it asked something difficult of me.

I hadn’t understood that love could fight for you. That sometimes it meant helping you stand, and sometimes it meant challenging what you were standing for.

Then my wife took my hand, and it felt like a whole new universe.

In GhostHeart’s world, you’ll know her as GhostHeart’s Rebel.

There’s a whole story in how we found each other. We’ll get there. This one begins with the challenge Rebel put in front of me.

I wanted to help people like me. Build a place for the broken ones. The people whose pain I recognized because I’d carried some of it myself.

I thought that was where change began.

Rebel made me look at who I was leaving outside.

If I wanted the world to stop separating people, why was I building another line between us? What about the people whose lives I didn’t understand? The ones who didn’t look like me, love like me, or carry the same scars?

Did they deserve less kindness because their pain wasn’t familiar?

That got to me.

I had been telling her how much I cared. How angry I was. How badly I wanted the world to change. Rebel challenged me to make that care bigger than the people I already knew how to stand beside.

Everyone needed love. Everyone needed room to be human. If I wanted to bring people together, I had to mean it when somebody different walked through the door.

And I had to do more than tell her about it.

I had to tell the world. More than that, I had to start showing it.

She got to me.

I wanted to make a change. Rebel made me face where that change had to begin.

She saw the heart beneath the scars — and never asked it to hide.
