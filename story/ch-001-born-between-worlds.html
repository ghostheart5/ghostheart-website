Born Between Worlds

Content and source note: This chapter discusses childhood sexual abuse, domestic abuse, and gambling. It is drawn from family accounts and official records.

Before anyone gave me a name, there was the heartbeat.

It moved through the dark in a steady rhythm, small but stubborn, carrying a life that knew nothing yet of the world waiting beyond it.

I remember none of my own arrival. My beginning comes through the memories of those who were there and the records that survived. Before my first breath, two families had already spent generations forming different ideas of love, marriage, safety, money, and survival.

Long before those histories became mine, they belonged to my parents.

• • •

My father was raised by a mother and father who stayed married. From the outside, that permanence may have resembled stability. Inside their home, there was fighting, anger, violence, chaos, and disrespect. The marriage endured, but safety did not. It was not a loving home.

As a boy, my father was sexually abused. He repressed what happened and carried it forward without the healing it required. Compassion belongs to the child he was; responsibility for the abuse he later inflicted belongs to the man he became.

His family lived with limited means, though money lost through gambling and casinos kept their life more unstable than it might otherwise have been. My paternal grandmother did not work outside the home and depended financially on her husband, limiting the choices available to her within an already unsafe household.

Very little reached me about who my father had been before he met my mother. I cannot honestly name many strengths from that part of his life. What survived most clearly was the world that formed him—a world where violence, instability, and disrespect had become familiar.

• • •

My mother came from a loving and respectful two-parent home. Her two older brothers protected her. She grew up knowing she belonged and believing that the people closest to her were supposed to provide safety.

She experienced pediatric health complications during infancy and had a neurodevelopmental learning disability. Neither diminished her worth or her place in the family.

Her mother worked throughout her life, valued independence, and dedicated herself to helping others. She fostered adult women with intellectual disabilities and made service part of the way she lived.

My maternal grandfather was a veteran who worked until a broken back left him disabled. He was deeply protective of my mother, sometimes overprotective. He never raised his voice or yelled at my grandmother. Their family took vacations and gathered for Sunday dinners, giving my mother a steady rhythm of belonging.

She entered adulthood expecting love and safety to exist together. Violence had never been normalized around her. She did not yet understand that some families lived inside fear or that a marriage could remain standing after respect disappeared. Her family gave her proof that storms were not the whole sky.

• • •

My parents met as teenagers in high school.

My mother was friends with my father’s sister, and that friendship brought their lives together. They dated for about a year, still close to the families that had shaped them and already making decisions that would affect the rest of their lives.

They entered the relationship carrying opposing definitions of home. My mother had learned that love created safety. My father had learned that a family could remain together while violence ruled inside it.

Then my mother became pregnant with my sister.

The pregnancy turned a teenage relationship into a decision involving both families. My father’s parents insisted that they marry before the baby was born. My maternal grandfather opposed the marriage. Their choice was made beneath the weight of pregnancy, family expectations, money, and loyalty.

My parents married before my sister was born. They became parents while they were still becoming adults themselves.

After my sister was born, my father was deployed overseas. His departure created a dividing line in the family story: the person who left and the person who returned.

The official record confirms that he received a dishonorable discharge from the military. When he came home, my mother said he had changed.

Only after his return did my father begin abusing her.

It was domestic abuse—direct and real, not an equal conflict between two people. My father inflicted it. My mother lived under it.

My brother was born after my father returned. By then, two children were living inside the consequences of a marriage shaped by two very different histories.

Then my mother became pregnant with me.

The pregnancy was unplanned. The news came as a surprise. There were no known prenatal complications, but the home waiting for me was already unsafe.

My father’s abuse of my mother was part of that home before I was born. It continued from my birth until I was seven years old. Its effects have shaped my entire life.

Domestic abuse does not remain sealed between two adults. It changes the emotional structure of a home and the meaning of words like safety, silence, love, and control. A child does not need to understand what is happening for fear to become part of daily life.

I was coming into the space between them.

• • •

I was born preterm, with a breech presentation.

Only my mother’s friend and the medical team were with her. My father was traveling by city bus, trying to reach the hospital.

My mother held me first.

That is the clearest image at the center of my arrival: the woman who carried me becoming the first person to hold me.

She had grown up surrounded by protection, hard work, family gatherings, and respect. By the time she held me, she was living a different reality.

The birth of a child did not make the abuse disappear. It did not repair the marriage or turn the home safe.

A baby cannot repair a family by arriving.

A baby can only arrive.

I entered the world with no understanding of the histories that had produced that moment. Still, all of it was waiting for me.

After the hospital, I was taken to an apartment. It became the first home of my life, though only briefly. We lived there for about two months and then moved.

I remember nothing of those rooms. Memory would come later. So would awareness.

The abuse was already there, moving through the home before I had words for home. I had no way to know how deeply those first years would follow me.

For now, there was only the heartbeat.

I am here. I am here. I am here.

Before the world gave me any other identity, I was simply a child arriving inside a divided inheritance.

I was born between worlds.

Family Context

Two family histories meet here.

Mother, father, siblings, and grandparents remain identified by relationship rather than names or locations.

Paternal family background

A two-parent household stayed together, but the home was not safe. Fighting, anger, violence, chaos, and disrespect shaped the environment GhostHeart’s father grew up in.

Maternal family background

A loving two-parent home was shaped by work, service, protection, family trips, Sunday dinners, and respect. GhostHeart’s mother grew up expecting love and safety to belong together.

The parents before Chapter One

They met as teenagers and married before their first child was born. They entered the relationship carrying very different understandings of home, and those histories became part of the world GhostHeart entered at birth.
