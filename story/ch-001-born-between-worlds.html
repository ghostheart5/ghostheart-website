Born Between Worlds

Before anyone gave him a name, there was the heartbeat. It moved through the dark in a steady rhythm, small but stubborn, as if it had already decided that whatever waited beyond the quiet would not be enough to erase it.

He came into a world divided. On one side were gentle hands, lamplight, and voices that lowered when he slept. His grandparents held him as though tenderness were not something a person had to earn. In their home, a clock kept patient time above the kitchen doorway, a kettle sang before sunrise, and the floorboards knew the weight of people who stayed.

Beyond that doorway, the other world was louder. Its moods changed without warning. Some days arrived warm and harmless; others split open like storms. Long before he understood the words adults used for fear, he learned its weather. He could hear tension inside a cupboard closing too hard. He could feel it gathering in the silence between two sentences. He watched faces the way sailors watch the sky.

So the boy became quiet. Not empty—never empty—but careful. He tucked his questions behind his teeth. He made himself small when the room demanded it and disappeared into the hidden country of his imagination when small was not small enough. There, shadows had names. Broken things could speak. A lonely heart could walk through fire and return carrying light.

At his grandparents’ table, he remembered that he was allowed to take up space. A chair waited for him. A plate was set down without conditions. When his grandfather laughed, it came from somewhere deep and certain. When his grandmother rested a hand between his shoulders, the noise inside him softened. They could not stop every storm, but they gave him proof that storms were not the whole sky.

Still, the two worlds followed him. He carried one in each hand: safety and uncertainty, warmth and warning, the child he was and the watchman he had become. People saw the quiet and mistook it for weakness. They did not see the work happening underneath—the counting of exits, the reading of eyes, the building of whole universes behind an expression that revealed almost nothing.

At night, when the house settled and even the walls seemed to breathe, he listened to his own pulse. It was the one sound that belonged completely to him. Not to the loud world. Not even to the safe one. Each beat said the same thing in a language older than fear: I am here. I am here. I am here.

Years would pass before he understood what that heartbeat had protected. The sensitivity he tried to hide would become the part of him that heard what others missed. The inner worlds he escaped into would become songs, images, and stories. The silence he once wore like armor would one day break open—not as a scream, but as a voice.

For now, he was only a child standing at the border, one foot in each world, learning that a person could be shaped by opposite things without belonging entirely to either. He did not know the road ahead. He did not know how many versions of himself he would have to become to walk it.

He did not know the name GhostHeart yet. But the heart was already there.
