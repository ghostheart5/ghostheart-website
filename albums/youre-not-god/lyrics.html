He wore a suit, she wore the white,
They stood beneath a church-house light.
The preacher spoke, the families cried,
They promised God they'd never divide.
But somewhere down those passing years,
Love turned to silence, trust to tears.
Two rings came off, two doors slammed shut,
And forever wasn't long enough.

Two young women met back when
The world was colder to them then.
No crowded church, no cheering room,
Just trembling hands and borrowed blooms.
They built a life through fifty years,
Through losing friends and burying fears.
Through sickness, bills, and growing old,
One kept the other when nights got cold.

So if Heaven saw the whole thing,
Not the names, not what we're told—

Or the one that stayed for fifty years
When staying was the hardest thing to do?
Would You count who held whose hand,
Or every promise they lived through?
If love is patient, love is kind—
Tell me, which love would You choose?

They've got silver in their hair now,
Photographs across the wall.
One still reaches for the other
Every time she starts to fall.
They've watched the whole world change outside,
Still wake up sleeping side by side.
Fifty winters, fifty springs—
Still wearing those two simple rings.

Would God turn from a lifetime

Tell me, which love would You choose?
