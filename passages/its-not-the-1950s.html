It’s Not the 1950s

Get email updates

Some lessons arrive as stories told across a kitchen table.

It’s Not the 1950s grows out of one of those. Grandma told it the way people tell things they have watched happen more than once: the government makes a law, the people push back, and years later the government takes the credit for the change the people fought for.

She was not talking about one fight. She was talking about a pattern.

Slavery did not end because the people in power woke up one day and decided it was wrong. People resisted, escaped, organized and refused to stop. Women did not get the vote because it was handed to them. They marched and argued and kept showing up long after they were told to go home. Same-sex couples did not win the right to marry because someone at the top changed their mind first. They lived openly, told their stories and fought in court until the law caught up.

Each time, the history books can make it sound like the change came from above. Grandma wanted me to remember where it really started.

The change was won by people. The credit was taken by power.

That is not a reason to give up on laws. Laws matter. They can protect people or leave them exposed. It is a reason to remember who moves them.

And the story is not finished. Women today are still fighting for the right to make their own choices about their own lives. Saying “it’s not the 1950s” is not the same as saying the work is done. It is a reminder that the calendar moving forward does not guarantee that freedom moves with it.

What Grandma passed on was not anger for its own sake. It was attention. Notice who is doing the fighting. Notice who shows up to take the bow. And when it is your turn, do not wait for permission to stand up for someone’s freedom, including your own.

The people who changed things were ordinary people who refused to stay quiet.

That door is still open to anyone willing to walk through it.
