Song reading · Journal entry October 10, 2026

Cruel — Something Needs to Change

Something needs to change.

Something needs to change.

We can’t keep watching people get hurt, shaking our heads, and carrying on like the way we treat each other has nothing to do with us.

There’s a person on the other side of what we’re watching. Someone who has to live with what happened long after everybody else has moved on. The video ends. The comments stop coming. That person still has to wake up tomorrow and carry it.

And sometimes, after enough of that, people start to believe nobody cares.

Maybe they reached out and got laughed at. Maybe they tried to explain and somebody decided they were being dramatic. Maybe they’ve heard “you should be over it” so many times that they’ve stopped telling anyone they’re still hurting.

Then we wonder why they shut down. Why they don’t trust anybody. Why kindness makes them hesitate, like there has to be something coming after it.

Trauma and life can break us. What happens to us can change how we see ourselves, what we expect from other people, and whether we believe it’s safe to let anyone close. We don’t get to look at somebody for a few seconds and pretend we understand everything that brought them there.

That’s what I want you to sit with when you hear Cruel.

Before we judge somebody’s reaction, can we care enough to wonder what they’re reacting to? Before we add our voice to a crowd laughing at someone, can we stop and think about what it would feel like to be the person everybody’s watching?

We have choices in those moments. We can make someone feel smaller. We can leave them standing there alone. Or we can do something that gives them a reason to question the belief that nobody cares.

Sometimes that means speaking up when someone is being humiliated. Sometimes it means asking a question and staying long enough to hear the answer. Sometimes it means admitting, “I don’t know how to help with all of this, but I’m listening.”

And if you say that, listen. Let them finish. Give them room to tell the truth without having to make it easier for you to hear.

We can’t undo everything that happened to somebody. One conversation won’t erase years of hurt. But the way we respond becomes part of what happens next. It can add to the damage, or it can give someone an experience of being treated with care.

Maybe that makes it a little easier for them to ask for help again. Maybe they leave that moment feeling less ashamed. Maybe, for once, they don’t have to defend why they’re hurting before someone will be kind to them.

Those things matter.

If you’re the person who feels like nobody cares, I want to say this to you: you shouldn’t have to fall apart in front of an audience for your pain to matter. You shouldn’t have to tell your whole story to earn basic kindness. What happened to you deserves to be taken seriously, even when you don’t have the words to explain it.

And for the rest of us, caring has to reach further than saying we care. It has to show up in how we speak, what we laugh at, what we allow around us, and how we treat someone when there’s nothing to gain from being decent.

Something needs to change. We can start with the next person in front of us. We can pay attention. We can choose our words with care. We can stand beside someone who’s being left out instead of waiting for somebody else to go first.

That’s the question I want to leave with you:

When somebody is already hurting, what will your response add to their life?

I want Cruel to make us think about that long after the song ends.
